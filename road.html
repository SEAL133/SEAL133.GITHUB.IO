The Road

What forged the commitment before the workings were written

Cartoons First

Sports held no interest growing up. Cartoons did. The games people gathered around on weekends were background noise to a kid whose attention belonged elsewhere. That is the honest starting point of this story, because what came later only means something against that baseline.

The things that forged the commitment were not chosen by preference. They arrived uninvited and stayed permanently. That pattern runs through everything on this page.

Showtime

1984 brought the Los Angeles Lakers — not because sports had suddenly become interesting, but because what was happening on that court was impossible to ignore. Magic Johnson and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar were not playing a game. They were demonstrating a standard. The Showtime era showed what it looked like when preparation, precision, and excellence in motion combined at the highest level. That demonstration landed and never left.

Excellence in motion looks different from excellence described. 1984 showed the difference.

The Commitment to Excellence

The Oakland Raiders of 1980 and 1981 were built on Al Davis's organizational credo and coached by Tom Flores — the first Mexican-American head coach in NFL history and the first minority head coach to win a Super Bowl. A son of California farmworkers who reached the highest level of his profession and brought a championship with him.

The Commitment to Excellence is not a quote. It is a slogan — a standard, a demand placed on every person who wore the silver and black. From 1984 forward, seeing the Raiders' presence in Downtown Los Angeles at the movies made it concrete. The slogan became part of the city's identity and quietly, of an interior architecture being built at the same time.

The Commitment to Excellence was not aspirational. It was operational. You either lived it or you did not belong there.

Years later, the Raiders played their final game in Los Angeles at the Memorial Coliseum. The date was December 24, 1994 — the 24th. That game was attended alone, with tickets found in the trash by a father who knew what they were worth. The last time that standard would be displayed in that city, witnessed by one person who had absorbed it as a way of life.

For those who know the Chaldean system: 24 is the compound of VICTORY and of CREATOR alike. The last game, the solo witness, the discarded tickets recovered — all of it carried that number. Not coincidentally.

All Glory to the Creator

The road described on this page was not walked alone. Credit belongs first and always to the One who sees in secret — the Creator whose governance over this life has been demonstrated beyond any reasonable doubt. The frameworks in the workings circle the same center from different distances. The center itself is not a framework.

And credit belongs to the people the Creator placed on the road at the right moments: to a Mother whose six words sealed more than she knew, and to a Godmother named Anne who was there when no one else was.

There are simply too many people, persons, and circumstances to give personal thanks to — each one known to the Creator, and each one honored in that knowledge.

The Commitment to Excellence was a slogan. What gave it lasting force was the knowledge that excellence is not performed for audiences — it is maintained in the temple, in the quiet, before the field is ever taken. That is the doctrine the workings carry. It was learned in a city, at a stadium on the 24th, in the silence that followed.
