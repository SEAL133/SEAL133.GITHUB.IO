The Author

No identification will be offered beyond the work itself. The work is the credential. The journey it describes is real and was lived before a single word of it was written down.

In Chaldean numerology, the author is a Number 1 — ruled by the Sun, governed by compound 19, the Prince of Heaven. In the language of the tradition: the House of the Rising Sun. The workings were built from that house and are given from it.

The Tao Te Ching was read many times without understanding, driven by an innate will to find the true meaning of its words. That understanding eventually arrived. The paradox it speaks of is no longer a text to be studied — it is a condition being lived.

The Quran was read in administrative segregation. Joseph Campbell's The Hero with a Thousand Faces — one of the most difficult reads of this life — was where the Hero's Journey was identified as the journey actually being walked. These were not academic exercises. They were the temple calculations of a man who had nowhere else to be and everything to figure out.

The seeking was the constant. It never occurred to me that the seeking might one day end in an arrival.

Half a lifetime ago, outside a Barnes & Noble, a friend named Mike asked a question that has never left: “What are you going to do? You can do something about it now — but is that really helping her? She has to live with that guy.” The lesson absorbed that day: if the problem cannot be fixed permanently, it is best to leave it alone. That principle became a foundation for everything that followed, including this work.

The Commitment to Excellence was not learned in a classroom. It was absorbed on the streets of Los Angeles, in movie theaters in Downtown LA, and at the Memorial Coliseum on December 24, 1994 — the last Raiders game in the city, attended alone, on tickets recovered from the trash.
